# Decision log

Newest last. Each entry: what was decided, why, and the measurement or fact behind it.

1. **Reads come from NCBI's copy on AWS when ENA is slow** (2026-11-06). ENA moved about 1 Mbit/s over Aspera and 0.3 MB/s over https from three unrelated networks (utaka, D07, a home connection) and four files, at the same time as the AWS copy
   ran at 17-40 MB/s on one connection. Not a Georgia Tech firewall problem; no request to IT. `--source auto` probes Aspera and orders the sources; the other is the fallback.
2. **Every SRA run is checked against ENA.** vdb-validate; fasterq-dump's spot count against ENA's `read_count`; the first 1,000 reads (sequence and quality) against ENA's FASTQ. NCBI also distributes SRA Lite files with simplified qualities.
3. **ENA's `read_count` has no single meaning** (2026-11-06). ERR13389718: 109,512,218 = 2 x 54,756,109 spots (reads). ERR14734229: 40,613,306 against 40,650,256 spots (about pairs). All 8 runs measured have 0.04-0.40% MORE spots in SRA than ENA's count.
   The check tries both readings and accepts up to 1% more, rejects more than 0.1% fewer. ReadLength comes from the Run Info's AvgSpotLen, not from a guess. (The tolerance became 5% in entry 31.)
4. **Binned qualities are not a sign of SRA Lite.** ERR13389718 (HiSeq 2500) has 6 distinct quality symbols at ENA and at NCBI. Only one or two distinct symbols is treated as Lite-like. No instrument-based rule.
5. **The sheet is written cell by cell, never by whole worksheet** (setSampleDatabase rewrites worksheets and flattens formulas; two machines would overwrite each other). Runs are claimed before work; a claim expires after 6 h; an unreachable sheet
   does not stop a download (journal, replayed later). (Refined by entry 30: whole-sheet rewrites are allowed for single-writer bulk tools.)
6. **`DownloadDatabase` is a spreadsheet of its own**, one tab per Run Info file, so nothing that rewrites the SampleDatabase or the alignment sheets can touch it.
7. **Speeds measured:** D07 11 MB/s (8 connections); mzebra 104 MB/s for one download, about 150 MB/s for three; utaka 40 MB/s (single connection, curl). A run's .sra file is 70% of its FASTQ size; fasterq-dump 38 s; the uBAM conversion 12 min (D07).
   The upload of the uBAMs to Dropbox (about 3.7 TB for the 328 runs) is the likely limit: stage timings are in the sheet since v9d.
8. **The B chromosome is found by replicating Clark et al.'s coverage ratio on the project reference, not by lifting his blocks** (56% of his core sequence is on unplaced contigs of the old assembly).
9. **B-chromosome reads are downloaded through the SRA source** (`bchrom` download.source: auto) with ENA as the fallback; disk is reserved at 9x the ENA FASTQ size for the SRA route (.sra + uncompressed FASTQ + scratch).
10. **One module, one folder, contracts instead of imports** (SPEC.md). A module may import the package of an upstream module it depends on, never its internals.

13. **Measurement and call are separate; the pilot sets the thresholds** (2026-11-08). A BAM is reduced to raw counts stored with the hash of the definition (`<sample>.panel.json`); genotypes come from a later call step, so a threshold can change without touching a BAM, and measurements made under different definitions are never mixed. LG5 (cuts 0.25 and 0.75) and the B chromosome (2.5 and 1.6) start with the midpoints of the expected values and are refit on the pilot cohort's own distributions.
14. **LG5 inversion: 954 interior SNV markers plus the right-breakpoint junction** (2026-11-08). GT3 carries the standard arrangement; neither OB assembly contains the standard interior. The right breakpoint (GT3 26,726,680) is unique sequence and genotypable (junction a and b present in the OB pools of both genera, 0 of 143 reads in BB pools); the left lies in a repeat. Markers: alleles shared by LatrZW and MezeOBm, clean in the BB pools and Luwino, at about the inverted fraction in the OB pools (held-out OB pool 0.53 and 0.57). Held-out BB-type pools are NOT clean (0.06-0.20), so a diploid fish is called on the median per-site fraction. SRR18083730 came back from ENA with no base qualities (Q0), so no base-quality filter is used anywhere.

22. **The gsdf junction cross-check expected the wrong magnitude and is to be replaced** (2026-11-08). It assumed unique junction coverage / unique flank coverage = (copies - 2) / 2 (0.5 for three copies, 1.0 for four). In the MC family (sheet copy numbers) the ratio is at most 0.054 in 25 two-copy samples and has median 0.243 (0 to 0.442, zero in at least 10%) in 21 three-copy samples, at a median flank depth of 5.8x (minimum 2.5x); a 4-copy fish at 18x (SAMEA117806076) gives 0.00. Reads wholly inside the 500 bp window map ambiguously (the copies are 99.4% identical), so only spanning reads count, and at low depth zero is common. The check only sets `check_flags`, never the call. Replaced (2026-11-08) by a one-sided rule: a unique-read junction ratio above 0.15 in a sample called with two copies is reported as `junction_in_two_copy` (all 25 two-copy MC samples were at or below 0.054); a missing junction in a sample called with three or more copies is not checked, and `junction_implied_copies` was removed. A depth-aware missing-junction rule stays open until well-covered samples are measured (the full run over the sheet's samples, not started).

45. **Every panel JSON records the BAM it was measured on** (2026-11-09; requested by 03, SPEC "Requests from 03 to 04"). Schema 2: `bam` is the file name and `bam_size_bytes` the size. Reason: samples are realigned when new runs are added and keep their file name (`<sample>.all.bam`), so the size is what tells a measurement of the old BAM from one of the new. The old fields could not do this: `bam` was an absolute path (specific to the machine) and, for samples measured through `retro`, pointed to a temporary regional BAM that is deleted afterwards, with that BAM's size. For streamed samples the identity is now that of the source BAM on Dropbox, read with an HTTP HEAD request (a one-byte range request if the server sends no length) before anything is fetched, so a missing BAM is reported as `no_bam` without a fetch. `bam_size` was renamed `bam_size_bytes`; schema 1 files are still read (a streamed one has no usable size: `unknown`); every call table carries the identity; `sd_genotypes.panel.identity` answers current / stale / unknown. Not done: `retro` still skips a sample that already has a JSON whatever BAM it was measured on (use `--refresh`); a name and a size are not a checksum (a header fingerprint or a checksum would be stronger).

A guard in the pipeline (`tools/env.sh` putting the active environment first, and a `--expect-tools` check) was built and **withdrawn at the lab's request**: the cause is a machine setting, and the pipeline stays simpler without it. The rule: activate the environment inside tmux, and check `which -a minimap2 samtools` on a new machine; a child shell re-runs conda's startup, so an environment activated outside tmux does not carry in.
58. **sexloci 0.1.26 becomes module 06, unchanged** (2026-11-10). It is the lab's package for mapping sex-linked loci from a single-species VCF (preflight rules, family-aware association, pedigree transmission, Fst and heterozygosity, tiers A, B and C, plots, `run-folder`). It contains no variant caller, only `prefilter.sh`. It is copied in unchanged with a checksum manifest (as the transposon sites were copied into 04), keeping the package name `sexloci`; its genotyping commands (gsdf, TE1, TE2, chr7ins and the guarded sheet writer) stay in the copy but are deprecated in favour of module 04. The simple `sex_association` and `parental_sex_region` of `YH_SD_Paper` are retired after parity on the positive controls. Its simulator recovered a planted XY locus (tier A, 3 of 3 families); its 105 tests were not run for lack of pytest.
59. **The cohort caller is bcftools, following Blumer** (2026-11-10). `bcftools mpileup` and `bcftools call -m` (the samtools suite) are the default for module 05, with Blumer's parameters: primary mapped, non-duplicate reads, no QC-fail flag, base quality 20 and mapping quality 13, heterozygosity prior 0.001, a Hardy-Weinberg prior within groups, indels needing 2 gapped reads and 5% of the reads, `norm` and `fill-tags` (Blumer et al. 2025 supplement; code cited there: `bef22/vcf_snakemake`). GATK stays a config option and is no longer the default. The VCF is raw plus annotation, FILTER labelled and no site removed. Consequence: sexloci's default filters use GATK annotations that bcftools does not write, so 06 needs a configuration for bcftools VCFs. Blumer's inbreeding-coefficient, excess-heterozygosity and low-depth filters are not applied to sex mapping, because they remove sex-linked sites.
60. **Cohorts only** (2026-11-10). One cohort is one call. Related-species groups are not called jointly and then split into per-species VCFs.
61. **Cohort rules depend on the kind of cohort** (2026-11-10). Module 05 checks a cohort before any calling, from the sheet and the ledger, with the rules of sexloci's preflight for `family` and `sex_set` cohorts. A third kind, `bc_parents` (the parents and F1s of the YH x MC backcross), is allowed to have two species, no minimum per sex and unknown sex, and requires a complete pedigree, alignments to the genome and enough coverage to call genotypes (threshold open). The BC parents VCF is for module 07: sexloci refuses crosses. The rules live in 05; sexloci is not modified.
62. **Mapping needs whole-genome VCFs** (2026-11-10). sexloci's permutation null is the genome-wide maximum and the BC analysis needs informative sites on every chromosome, so cohort VCFs cover the whole genome. The slices of LG2, LG10 and LG13 are for targeted genotyping, not for mapping; an earlier suggestion that slices could make cheap regional VCFs for mapping is withdrawn.
63. **Lab and farm stock are called without a shared Hardy-Weinberg prior** (2026-11-10; refines decision 59). Blumer et al. called wild-caught fish with a Hardy-Weinberg prior within clades. The lab lines, vendor stocks and BC-cross parents are inbred with long runs of homozygosity, so heterozygotes are far rarer than the prior expects and false heterozygotes are favoured; the worst case is two lines and their F1s in one group. Default for module 05: each sample its own group (`bcftools call -G -`; to be checked in the pinned version). Clade groups remain an option for wild-caught cohorts.
64. **A run-of-homozygosity track is made for every fish** (2026-11-10). Heterozygous calls inside a ROH are enriched for collapsed paralogs and mapping errors. 05 finds ROH from the density of heterozygous sites in windows (Blumer's windowed heterozygosity: 1 Mbp windows, 10 kb step; window size and threshold open), with `bcftools roh` as the alternative to compare. Each heterozygous genotype inside a ROH is annotated, none is removed, and the manifest reports each fish's ROH fraction.
65. **Heterozygotes are filtered per genotype, and no population filter is applied to sites** (2026-11-10). A heterozygous genotype becomes missing when its allele balance is biased (Blumer's binomial test, PHRED 20) or its depth is below a heterozygote minimum (values open). Inbreeding-coefficient, excess-heterozygosity, Hardy-Weinberg and low-depth site filters are not used: random mating is false for these fish and they remove sex-linked sites. Heterozygosity is annotated per sex, so a paralog artifact (heterozygous in both sexes) can be told from a sex-linked region (one sex).
66. **A GT3 accessibility mask is built once and applied to every cohort** (2026-11-10). After Blumer's QC subset: from the deepest samples across ecogroups, sites with depth outside the 95% interpercentile range, RMS mapping quality below 35, MQ0 or BQ0 fraction above twice the genome average, RMS base quality below 20, or no reads, and sites whose VCF depth is outside the median plus or minus 25%, become FILTER labels. Built by 05 or 01 and from which samples: open; cost unverified.
67. **Relatedness and inbreeding are checked before a cohort reaches module 06** (2026-11-10). The cohort check warns from the sheet's brood and parent columns when a `sex_set` pool holds siblings or parent and offspring; `qc` computes genotype-based kinship and F_ROH per sample and warns when F_ROH differs between the sexes, because male-female heterozygosity then differs for reasons unrelated to sex determination. sexloci is unchanged and does not check inbreeding, so the guard is in 05. Pedigree mapping has fewer informative markers with inbred parents (power, not error).
68. **The calling settings are chosen by a brood benchmark** (2026-11-10). On YH-22-09-06 (24 offspring, 2 parents): the shared prior, groups by line and no shared prior, with the heterozygosity prior at one lower and one higher value (values open), scored by Mendelian error where both parents are homozygous, false heterozygotes, missed heterozygotes, heterozygous calls inside ROH, concordance with the GATK VCF, and what 06 reports. Nothing here is tested yet; the default of decision 63 stands until the benchmark says otherwise.
69. **Inversion genotyping by PCA does not follow Blumer's three-step method** (2026-11-10). The lab does not want to follow it. Decision 54's PCA basis panel remains the plan, and its method is to be specified with the student. Blumer's steps (PCA I on benthic samples to pick the two homozygous sets, PCA II on those sets, PCA III on the sites variable between them, over biallelic variants without missing genotypes in each inversion, checked against PCR on transposon insertions) are recorded here only as what was not adopted.

74. **Future work: tell optical from library (PCR) duplicates, and calculate the quality metrics per run** (2026-11-11; recorded, not built, not before the current alignment batches have finished). Found while analysing the effective coverage of 566 samples. (1) The pipeline runs `gatk MarkDuplicates` with defaults: the optical pixel distance is 100 (the setting for unpatterned flow cells; GATK's documentation gives 2500 for patterned ones, which carry most of the data: HiSeq X, NovaSeq 6000, NovaSeq X), the read-name pattern is the Illumina default (Element AVITI names may not parse, unverified) and nothing is tagged, so the BAM does not say which duplicates are optical; `OpticalDuplicateFraction` exists only in `qc.json`, only for the samples the new pipeline aligned (9 of the 566, all clean libraries). The total duplicate rate is unaffected; only the classification. (2) The rate of a sample averages its runs, which can behave very differently: in the NewAulonocara project the 42 resequenced samples (a second run named `_extra_coverage` on Element AVITI, the same LibraryID as the first, a median 63% of the sample's data by file size) have a median of 20.0% duplicates against 34.6% for the 73 single-run samples, the second run's share of the data correlates with the merged rate at r = -0.94, and a back-calculation (assuming the first run behaves like the single-run samples) puts the second run's own rate near 11%; in the MC_YH_Hybrids the AVITI top-up of the PM26 batch took the same kind of sample from about 3.5% to about 44%, so the instrument is not the cause and the merged BAM cannot show what is. Wanted: the pixel distance and read-name pattern per instrument and `--TAGGING_POLICY All` (the BAM then carries `DT:SQ` for optical and `DT:LB` for library duplicates); every duplicate and quality figure per run (read group): rate with its optical and library shares, MAPQ pass fraction, insert size and mate overlap, base survival and yield, in `qc.json` and as a run-level table; a backfill for the existing BAMs, which needs the run to be in the read group (unverified). Duplicates between two runs of one library can only be library duplicates, because an optical duplicate needs the same tile. Open decision 22 of the spec: where the metrics per run are stored.
75. **Effective coverage is measured with the variant caller's own pileup and written to the AlignmentDatabase** (2026-11-11). Module 05's `effcov` downloads each BAM once and measures: `EffectiveCoverage` (the mean FORMAT/DP of `bcftools mpileup` run exactly as `cohort build` runs it, over one 1 Mb window on each of the 22 chromosomes, at the positions the caller emits), `MapqPassFraction` (reads with MAPQ >= 13 among the mapped, non-duplicate primary reads), `BaseSurvivalFraction` (the share of the bases the caller keeps after base quality, BAQ and mate overlap) and `DuplicationRate`, defined as primary duplicates over primary MAPPED reads, which is the definition of the pipeline's own flag-based value and, to within pairing, of Picard's, so the sheet's column is consistent (the older all-primary-reads fraction is lower by the unmapped share and is not written). The writer is `common/cichlid_common/sheet_writer.py`: a plan by default, only the named cells, empty cells only (a different value is left alone and reported unless `--overwrite`), the columns added at the end of the header, a read-back check and a journal; `UsableCoverage` is replaced by `EffectiveCoverage`, and `UsableCoverage`, `NRuns`, `Instrument` and `ReadLength` are dropped from the sheet **only after batches 3 and 4 have finished and the new code is deployed**: a running pipeline refuses to write a row when the header lacks a column it writes (`ReadLength` for the legacy `Coverage` comes from the reads database, not from this sheet). The legacy `Coverage` stays: sexloci compares the VCF depth with it. Why: the table's `Coverage` is raw yield, and the depth the caller sees ranges from 0.38 to 0.94 of it between libraries (the 24 saulosi and the NewAulonocara project lose most), so it cannot tell which samples reach sexloci's minimum depth of 5. For samples below 2x raw (all MC_YH_Hybrids) the effective depth is not comparable with the raw coverage (a covered position has depth >= 1); their covered fraction is the usable number. Measured for 566 of the 579 selected samples at the time of writing; the SD panel results for the SampleDatabase are open decision 23 of the spec.
